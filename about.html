How to Use This Site

This site is for practicing go/baduk/weiqi josekis. Don't know what go is? Get started here!

The board initially starts empty. From this point, you can play the first move of a joseki and the computer will play out a random follow-up from Josekipedia. The computer will only play follow-ups of the types selected under “Computer Plays” (ideal, good, bad, trick and questionable moves).

If you wish to play as White, select that under “Player”. The computer will make Black's moves.

If you wish to practice a specific joseki, select it under “Starting Point”, then click “Start”.

This site is best used by knowing which joseki you'd like to practice in advance, selecting it, and playing against the computer, trying to remember what the best moves are.

Modes

Explore: play a joseki against the computer from a starting point.

Problems: solve problems taken from the selected starting point. Choose which types you want to see:

  • Next Move: you're shown a position partway through a joseki and have to find the correct next move.
  • Punish: the computer makes a mistake or plays a trick move, and you have to find the move that punishes it.
  • Final Shape: faded stones show the shape a joseki ends in, and you have to play it out move by move while the computer plays the other side. Any move order that reaches the same shape counts. Show Answer highlights your next move.
  • Pick the Path: several numbered sequences are shown, and only one is joseki with both sides playing joseki moves. The others go wrong somewhere: a mistake recorded in Josekipedia, or a move that isn't joseki. After you answer, each one explains where it went wrong.

A wrong answer is taken back so you can try again. Show Answer highlights the correct moves, and after answering you can keep playing the position out against the computer. Press N for a new problem, or turn on Auto-advance to move on automatically after solving one. The Demo Board is only available in Explore mode.

Reviewing Problems You Miss

When you get a problem wrong on your first try (or use Show Answer), it's saved for review. It goes straight into your review queue; each time you then solve it first try, it waits longer before coming back: 1 hour, 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, then 1 year. Missing it during a review brings it back after 5 minutes.

Review mode works through everything that's due, whatever its type or starting point; you play each one as the color you missed it with. In Problems mode, Next also mixes due reviews in with new problems (about one in three), choosing ones that match your current problem types, player color and starting point. Your review deck is saved in this browser; you can look through it and remove problems on the Saved Reviews page (also linked from Review mode).

Tips

  • Use ← and → to undo and redo, and H to show the joseki moves for the current position, colored by type.
  • Right-click a stone to return to that move. If it's the computer's turn there, it will pick a new (possibly different) reply.
  • Click a coordinate like Q16 in a move's comment to mark it on the board with the matching shape (△, □ or ○); click again to remove it. Hovering shows it too.
  • In Explore mode, the page's address always leads back to the current position, so you can bookmark it or press Copy link to share it. Whoever opens the link plays the side to move.
  • Turn on Show follow-up variations to see the final shape each joseki move leads to, with Josekipedia's verdict on the result. The circled stone is the move that starts each variation.

Your Website is Broken

This site pulls all its data from Josekipedia, so if Josekipedia is down, the site won't be able to load joseki data. Please check that it's up before reaching out.
